You get recommended by ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity or Gemini the same way you get recommended by a well-informed friend: by being easy to find, easy to understand and vouched for by other people. There is no form to submit and no placement to buy. What you can do is remove every reason an assistant might have to skip you.

Can an AI assistant understand what you do?

Start with being understood. An assistant recommends the business it can describe with confidence. Your site should state in plain sentences what you do, who you do it for, where you work and what it costs or how pricing works. If a stranger cannot summarise your business after reading your home page for ten seconds, neither can a machine.

An assistant recommends the business it can describe with confidence.

Can it read your website?

Then make the site readable. That means fast pages, real text instead of words baked into images, a clear heading on every page, and structured data that labels your business, services and frequently asked questions. Check that your site is not blocking AI crawlers by accident. Some are, through a security setting nobody remembers switching on.

Do you answer the questions buyers ask?

Answer the questions buyers actually ask. Assistants are asked things like how much a service costs, how long it takes, and who is good at it in a given city or industry. A page that answers one of those directly, in its opening lines, is far more quotable than a page of slogans. Question-style headings help, because they match the way people phrase prompts.

Who else vouches for you?

Get mentioned in places you do not control. Reviews on Google and industry platforms, answers on Reddit and Quora, local news, supplier and partner pages, podcast notes. Assistants weigh what the wider web says about you more heavily than what you say about yourself, in the same way a person would.

Do your facts agree everywhere?

Keep the facts consistent. Name, services, location, hours and pricing should read the same on your site, your Google Business Profile, your social profiles and every directory. Conflicting details make an assistant hedge, and a hedged answer usually names someone else.

Does being on Google still matter?

Yes. Most assistants can search the live web when they answer, and they lean on pages that already rank and read clearly. A business that is invisible on Google is usually invisible to assistants too. Treat search and AI answers as one job with two outputs.

What does not work?

Stuffing a page with keywords does not work. Neither does hiding text, publishing dozens of thin pages, or writing copy addressed to the machine instead of the reader. Assistants are built to summarise what a careful reader would take from a page. Anything a careful reader would find odd, an assistant tends to discount.

A one-week checklist

  • Write one sentence that says what you do, who for and where, and put it at the top of your home page
  • Give each service its own page or section, with the price or how pricing works
  • Add the five questions buyers ask most, each with a two-sentence answer
  • Check that your name, services, hours and location match on your site and your Google Business Profile
  • Ask three recent customers for a review
  • Ask three assistants who they would recommend for what you do, and write down the answer

How long until it changes?

Then test it. Once a month, ask three assistants the questions your customers ask and write down who gets named. It will not change overnight. Assistants refresh what they know on their own schedule, so expect weeks to months. The businesses that show up a year from now are the ones that started tidying up today.
