The traditional pipeline is a relay race with hand-offs: strategy passes to design, design passes to motion, motion passes to development, and every baton drop takes a week of meetings. The work is not slow. The waiting is.

Our loop puts one small team around one table for five days. Day one is the moodboard and the single sentence the site has to feel like. Day two is type and grid. Day three is the hero in the browser, moving. Day four is the first full page. Day five is the client sitting in front of a working site instead of a deck about one.

Speed does not come from cutting steps. It comes from removing the waiting between them.

Speed does not come from cutting steps. It comes from removing the waiting between them. Generated imagery removes the waiting for a shoot. Hand-coded motion removes the waiting for a developer to interpret a designer's video. A client who sees the real thing on day five gives better feedback than one who sees a mock on day forty.

The five days are not the whole project. They are the part that decides whether the project is right. Everything after them is refinement, which is the part that should take time.
