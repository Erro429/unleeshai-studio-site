A landing page is one page with one job: take a visitor who arrived from a specific place and get them to do one specific thing. We design and hand-code landing pages for ad campaigns, email campaigns, launches and lead magnets. Each one is built around a single offer, a single action and nothing that distracts from either.

If you are paying for clicks, the quiet fear is that the money is leaking somewhere between the ad and the enquiry. It usually is, and it usually leaks on the page. A home page asks visitors to find what they came for. A landing page hands it to them.

What you get

  • One page planned around one offer and one call to action
  • A headline that matches the ad or email that sent the visitor, so the click feels continuous
  • Proof placed where the doubt appears: reviews, numbers, guarantees, answers to objections
  • A form, booking calendar or checkout that works on a phone with one thumb
  • A thank-you step that tells people what happens next
  • Tracking on the page so you can see how many visitors act, not only how many arrive
  • Fast load times, because every second of delay costs paid traffic

How we build it

We start with the offer, not the layout. What is being promised, to whom, and what would stop them saying yes. Then we plan the path from the first line to the button, and cut anything that does not move a visitor along it. Navigation, extra links and secondary offers all come out, because every extra link is a way to leave without acting.

The page is hand-coded, so there is no page-builder weight slowing it down. One of our live builds is exactly this kind of page: a single-offer landing page for an owner-financed land company, shown on the Work page.

For scale, a full five-page website starts at $499 and goes live in two to four days. The price and the ship date for your page are written into the quote before any work starts.

Landing page or website?

You need a website when people are checking whether you are real. You need a landing page when you are sending traffic somewhere and want it to do one thing. Most businesses end up with both. The comparison is laid out in landing page vs website. When a landing page needs follow-up emails and a measured path behind it, that is a sales funnel.

Common questions

What is a landing page?

A landing page is a single web page built around one offer and one action, such as requesting a quote, booking a call or buying a product. Visitors usually arrive on it from an ad, an email or a social post.

How is a landing page different from a home page?

A home page introduces the whole business and lets visitors explore. A landing page removes navigation and side offers on purpose so the visitor does one thing.

Do I need a website before I get a landing page?

No. A landing page can stand alone for a campaign. A full website helps when people look you up before they buy, which is why most businesses end up with both.

Can you write the copy for the page?

Yes. We write the headline, the offer and the body copy, or tighten what you already have. Copy is part of the build, not an extra.
