Pinned sections, scrubbed timelines and parallax have become a dialect. Used badly, they turn a page into a theme park ride where every element must be endured before the content appears. Used well, they let the page control pace the way an editor controls a cut.

Our test for every scroll-driven beat is simple: does the reader know something at the end of it that they did not know at the start? A cube that assembles from six faces while six capabilities light up teaches the structure of the studio. A word that bounces because it can teaches nothing. If the animation can be removed without losing meaning, remove it.

If the animation can be removed without losing meaning, remove it.

The second test is reversibility. Anything the reader scrolls into, they will scroll out of. If the piece only works forward, it is a video, not an interaction. Every scrubbed tween on this site plays backwards cleanly, which is a discipline that costs time and pays back in trust.

The third test is the one most sites fail: the page must still read as a page. Motion is punctuation. When every sentence ends in an exclamation mark, the reader stops hearing any of them.
