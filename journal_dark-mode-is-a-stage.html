Most dark modes are light modes with the values flipped. The same grid, the same card borders, the same drop shadows now pointing nowhere. The result is a site that looks like it is wearing sunglasses indoors.

A true dark design starts from a stage, not a page. The background is not a colour; it is the absence of light. Anything that appears is lit deliberately, and anything unlit does not exist. On a dark stage the light is the layout: the image with the single key light, the paragraph in soft white, the one hairline that separates two thoughts.

On a dark stage the light is the layout.

This changes small decisions. Cards lose their borders because a border is a lit edge with nothing behind it. Text goes one step brighter than instinct suggests, because grey on black reads as dust, not hierarchy. Video plates run at a third of their brightness so the type in front of them stays legible.

The reward is that the content gets to be the only bright thing in the room. That is a kind of attention a light background can never give it.
