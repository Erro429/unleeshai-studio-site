An audit is a week spent reading everything the brand has ever published: the site, the decks, the packaging, the support emails, the LinkedIn posts written at 11pm. We are not looking for mistakes. We are looking for the place where the brand tells the truth by accident.

Every audit finds the same thing: the brand is saying three things and believing one. The site claims innovation, the sales deck claims reliability, and the founder, when you get them talking, cares about craft. The audit names the one and gives the other two a graceful exit.

Every audit finds the same thing: the brand is saying three things and believing one.

Skipping the audit does not save the week. It moves the week to the middle of the project, when the design is half built and someone senior finally articulates what the company is actually for. Everything built before that sentence gets rebuilt. We have never seen an exception.

The audit ends in a single page: what the brand is, what it is not, and what the site therefore has to feel like. That page is the brief. Everything after it is execution.
