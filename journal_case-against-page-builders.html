Page builders solved a real problem: most businesses need a website, and a custom build used to cost more than they could justify. We are not arguing with that. We are arguing with the premium skincare house, the boutique clinic, the machining firm charging for tolerances no competitor can hit, when they ship a site that shares a skeleton with a dropshipping store.

You cannot charge a premium for a product that arrives in the same box as everyone else's. Visitors do not consciously recognise the template, but they recognise the rhythm: the hero, the three-column features, the testimonial slider, the footer. It reads as generic before a single word is processed, and generic is the opposite of what a premium price is asking the customer to believe.

You cannot charge a premium for a product that arrives in the same box as everyone else's.

There is a technical cost too. Builders ship megabytes of code to support features you did not use, and every animation runs through three layers of abstraction. The result is a site that is slower than it looks and harder to change than it should be. The debt arrives at the worst moment, usually a rebrand.

A hand-coded site is not a luxury. It is the only way to make the page do exactly one thing, feel exactly one way, and load exactly as fast as it should. For a brand that sells on precision, anything less is a contradiction on the front door.
