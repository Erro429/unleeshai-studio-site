Ask a cinematographer what makes a frame feel expensive and they will not talk about the camera. They will talk about where the light comes from, how hard it is, and what it refuses to touch. Most product imagery on the web is lit from everywhere, because that is what a white studio and a ring light do. It looks clean and it looks like nothing.

Our rule is one key light, always slightly above and to the side, with a long falloff into near-black. Every still and every loop on this site obeys it. When a client sends us a reference that breaks the rule, we shoot their version and ours and let them compare. Ours wins because it looks like it belongs with the rest of their brand, even when the rest of their brand does not exist yet.

Consistency is not a style. It is the absence of accidents.

The constraint matters even more for generated imagery. A diffusion model will happily give you forty product shots with forty different suns. Describing the light in words, in every prompt, is the difference between a catalogue and a pile of pictures. We write the light before we write the product.

Consistency is not a style. It is the absence of accidents. One key light removes the accidents so that the actual decisions, the material, the angle, the negative space, have room to be seen.
