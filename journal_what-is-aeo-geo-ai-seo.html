AEO stands for answer engine optimization. It is the work of getting your business named, quoted or linked when someone asks an AI assistant a question instead of typing it into a search box. The assistants in question are ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and the AI Overviews that now sit at the top of many Google results.

You will hear the same job called by other names. GEO means generative engine optimization. AIO usually means AI Overview optimization, or simply AI optimization. AI SEO and AI search optimization are the plain versions. They overlap almost completely. We say AEO and AIO because those are the terms our clients arrive with, but if an agency sells you GEO, it is describing the same work.

Search engines hand out a list. Answer engines hand out a recommendation. You want to be the recommendation.

The difference from classic SEO is the shape of the result. Search engines hand out a list. Answer engines hand out a recommendation. On a results page you can win a click from position four. In an AI answer there is often no position four: the assistant names two or three businesses and the conversation moves on.

So what does the work look like? First, the site has to be readable by machines: clean HTML, fast pages, and structured data that says plainly who you are, what you sell and where you work. Second, the content has to answer real questions directly. A page that opens with a clear answer in its first two sentences is easy to quote. A page that opens with three paragraphs of mood is not.

Third, the facts about your business have to match everywhere they appear: your site, your Google Business Profile, directories, review sites and social profiles. An assistant that finds three different descriptions of what you do will trust none of them. Fourth, other people need to mention you. Reviews, forum threads, local press and industry roundups all count, because assistants lean on what the wider web says about a business, not only on what the business says about itself.

AEO does not replace SEO. It sits on top of it. Most assistants can look things up on the live web when they answer, so a page that ranks well and reads clearly has a head start. That is why we build both into a site from the first commit instead of treating AI search as a separate project.

A quick test you can run today: open two or three assistants and ask the question your best customer would ask before hiring someone like you. Note who gets named. If it is not you, read what those businesses publish and where they are mentioned. That gap is the work.
