Every square inch of a page has a cost, and every stakeholder knows it. So the page fills. Another feature, another badge, another call to action just in case. Each addition is defensible and the sum is a site that looks like it is shouting from behind a market stall.

Premium brands work the other way. A luxury boutique puts one bag on a shelf built for twenty. The empty shelf is the message: we do not need to sell you this. Negative space on a page works the same way. It says the brand is not anxious, and an unanxious brand is one the customer can trust with a premium price.

Restraint is expensive to produce and cheap to recognise.

Restraint is expensive to produce and cheap to recognise. It takes a week of argument to remove the feature grid and a fraction of a second for the visitor to feel the difference. That asymmetry is the entire business case.

The measure we use is simple: if a section were removed, would anyone notice? Every section that fails the test comes out. What remains has room to be looked at, which is the only thing a premium page needs to do.
