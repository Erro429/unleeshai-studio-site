Every week somebody sends us a generated product image and asks why theirs looks like a stock photo while ours looks like a campaign. The honest answer is that we threw away twenty frames to keep one, and we knew what we were looking for before we started.

A commercial shoot has a brief, a lighting plan, a shot list and an art director on set who says no. Generation removes the set and the crew but not the need for the other four. Our prompts read like lighting plans: the key light, the surface, the exact distance from lens to object, what stays out of frame. The model gives you a draft. The art director gives you a shoot.

The model gives you a draft. The art director gives you a shoot.

Then there is the part nobody mentions: the retouch. Generated frames arrive with wrong reflections, invented text and lens behaviour that no lens has. We clean every frame at pixel level before it ships. A keycap with a legible, real character on it is worth more than a hundred with alphabet soup.

The result is imagery that survives being placed next to real photography, which is the only test that matters. When a client cannot tell which frames were shot and which were generated, we have done the job.
