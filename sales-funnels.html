A sales funnel is the path a stranger takes to become a customer, mapped out and built on purpose. In practice it is a short chain: something that brings the right people in, a page that makes one offer, a way to act on it, and follow-up for the people who are not ready yet. We design and build that chain and we measure each link.

Every business already has a funnel. Most were built by accident: someone hears about you, searches your name, lands on a home page, hunts for a phone number and maybe calls. Each step loses people. The quiet fear is not knowing which step, and the honest answer is that without measurement nobody knows.

What we build

  • The offer: what a visitor gets in exchange for a click, an email address or a call
  • A landing page built around that one offer
  • The capture: a form, a booking calendar or a checkout that works on a phone
  • A thank-you step that sets the next action instead of ending the conversation
  • A short follow-up sequence that answers the questions people have before they buy
  • Tracking at every stage, so the biggest drop is visible

The offer matters more than the pages

A beautiful page with a weak offer converts worse than a plain page with a strong one. So we start by pressure-testing the offer: is it worth an email address, is it clear in one sentence, does it match what the ad or the search promised. Only then do we draw anything.

Follow-up is where the money is

Few people buy on the first visit. A funnel without follow-up pays to attract visitors and then lets nearly all of them leave with no way to reach them again. Three or four honest emails that answer real objections will often do more than another month of ad spend.

Measuring it

Four numbers, tracked separately: how many people arrive, how many act on the offer, how many respond to follow-up, how many become customers. The step with the biggest drop gets fixed first. Without those four numbers every change is a guess.

Pricing

A funnel is quoted at a fixed price once we know the offer, the number of steps and what the capture has to connect to, such as your CRM, your calendar or your email tool. Getting visitors into the top of it is covered in traffic generation. The plain-words explainer is what a sales funnel is.

Common questions

What is a sales funnel?

A sales funnel is the sequence of steps that turns a stranger into a customer: attention, an offer, a way to act on it, and follow-up. Building one on purpose means each step can be measured and improved.

Does a small business need a sales funnel?

Yes, because it already has one. The question is whether it was designed or happened by accident. A simple funnel with a clear offer, a working form and a few follow-up messages is enough for most small businesses.

What is the difference between a landing page and a sales funnel?

A landing page is one page. A funnel is the whole path: the landing page, the capture, the thank-you step and the follow-up that comes after.

Do I need special funnel software?

No. You need a page with one clear offer, a form that works and a way to send follow-up messages. We build the pages and wire them to the tools you already use.
