The brief for Nocturne was unusual: the room is too dark to photograph, and the chef did not want plated shots because the plates change nightly. The site would have to be almost entirely typographic. We took that as a gift.

A menu is read slowly, by candlelight, by someone who wants to be persuaded. We set the type to match: a wide display face for course names, a light body face for descriptions, generous leading, and one hairline between courses. Nothing bold, nothing coloured, nothing that raises its voice.

A menu is read slowly, by candlelight, by someone who wants to be persuaded.

Motion carries the mood the imagery cannot. Each course reveals line by line as the reader scrolls, the way a waiter describes a dish. The reservation flow lives inside the same rhythm, so booking a table feels like reading to the end of the menu rather than leaving the restaurant to fill in a form.

The lesson we took away: a page does not need a picture to have an atmosphere. It needs a pace, and type sets pace better than anything else on the screen.
